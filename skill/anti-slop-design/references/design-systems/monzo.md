# DESIGN.md — Monzo (observed analysis)

> Hand-written analysis for this skill, from Monzo's public site and
> app. Tokens are observed / inferred hypotheses, not an official
> brand document. Do not copy Monzo's copy, logo, or layouts.
> Source: https://monzo.com

## 1. Overview
Monzo's system makes a bank feel like a friend: hot coral as
identity, plain English, and the app's real data as the design.

## 2. Brand personality
Friendly, honest, plain-speaking. It explains money in words a
person would actually use.

## 3. Colour (observed)
- Background: white `#FFFFFF`, light grey `#F2F2F2`.
- Text: dark navy `#14233C`, secondary grey.
- Accent: hot coral `#FF4F7B` (the card and the brand mark) used
  sparingly; navy for trust, coral for the action.
- Rule: one loud colour is the identity. Everything else stays quiet
  so it can be loud.

## 4. Typography
- Display and body: a friendly grotesque, clear, generous.
- Numbers are set large and clearly — money is the content.

## 5. Spacing and layout
- Clean cards for real transactions, clear hierarchy, the balance
  is the biggest number on the screen.

## 6. Components
- Cards, pots, and transaction rows — each one is real data, not a
  placeholder. Buttons are solid coral or navy.

## 7. Imagery and iconography
- Simple, flat illustrations in brand colours, used rarely. Icons
  are consistent and plain.

## 8. Motion
- Subtle and quick. Money moving is shown clearly, not decorated.

## 9. Voice and copy
- Plain English, first-person, honest. "Your money, sorted." Fees
  and terms are explained, not hidden.

## Do / Don't
- Do: let one colour be the identity, write like a person.
- Don't: copy the coral card, the transaction UI, or the copy.
- Take for your work: identity from one colour, trust from plain words.

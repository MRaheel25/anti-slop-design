# DESIGN.md — IKEA (observed analysis)

> Hand-written analysis for this skill, from IKEA's public site and
> catalogues. Tokens are observed / inferred hypotheses, not an
> official brand document. Do not copy IKEA's illustrations, copy,
> logo, or layouts. Source: https://www.ikea.com

## 1. Overview
IKEA's system is functional and democratic: blue and yellow as a
system, flat illustration, and instructions as design. It shows how
something works, in pictures, with almost no words.

## 2. Brand personality
Practical, optimistic, for everyone. It solves a problem in the
room, not in a slogan.

## 3. Colour (observed)
- Background: white `#FFFFFF`, warm off-white.
- Text: black `#000000` (used as a deliberate field) and dark grey.
- Accents: blue `#0058AB` and yellow `#FFDB00` — the flag colours,
  used as a system: blue for structure, yellow for the action or
  the highlight.
- Rule: two colours, each with a job, used flat.

## 4. Typography
- Display and body: a clear, friendly grotesque (IKEA Sans / Verdana
  heritage), large for prices, regular for instructions.
- Prices are the biggest numbers on the page, set plainly.

## 5. Spacing and layout
- Room scenes, product grids, and step-by-step instructions. The
  layout teaches: picture, step, result.

## 6. Components
- Price tags, room cards, and instruction diagrams. Every component
  shows a real product in a real room.

## 7. Imagery and iconography
- Flat illustrations and real room photography. Instruction diagrams
  use numbered steps and clear arrows — the only place arrows belong.

## 8. Motion
- Minimal on the site; the product's assembly is the motion, shown
  step by step.

## 9. Voice and copy
- Short, practical, second-person. Product names are real names.
  Instructions use verbs and pictures, not paragraphs.

## Do / Don't
- Do: show how it works in pictures, give each colour a job.
- Don't: copy the blue-yellow block, the instruction style, or the
  copy. Two flat colours are a system, not a costume.
- Take for your work: instructions as design, price as hierarchy.

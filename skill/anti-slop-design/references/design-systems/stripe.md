# DESIGN.md — Stripe (observed analysis)

> Hand-written analysis for this skill, from Stripe's public site and
> documentation. Tokens are observed / inferred hypotheses, not an
> official brand document. Do not copy Stripe's diagrams, copy, logo,
> or layouts. Source: https://stripe.com

## 1. Overview
Stripe's system earns trust from developers and finance teams by
being precise: proof, diagrams, and code before adjectives.

## 2. Brand personality
Precise, calm, competent. It explains clearly and proves quickly.

## 3. Colour (observed)
- Background: white `#FFFFFF`, light grey `#F6F9FC`.
- Text: dark slate `#0A2540`, secondary `#425466`.
- Accent: brand purple used sparingly as a brand mark, not as a
  gradient wash; product colours (green, blue, orange) each mean
  one thing in diagrams.
- Rule: colour means something — a product, a state, a code token —
  never decoration.

## 4. Typography
- Display: a grotesque with tight, confident headlines.
- Body: highly legible, generous line height, code in monospace.
- Hierarchy by weight and size; code samples are first-class content.

## 5. Spacing and layout
- Clean grid, generous whitespace, diagrams that explain the product
  in place. Sections alternate text and visual, never the same card
  row twice.

## 6. Components
- Buttons: solid dark or brand colour, rectangular with small radius.
- Cards are used for real content (pricing, docs), not as decoration.

## 7. Imagery and iconography
- Custom diagrams and product UI screenshots, drawn in the brand's
  visual language. No generic stock photos.

## 8. Motion
- Subtle, purposeful: diagrams animate to explain a flow.

## 9. Voice and copy
- Plain, specific, developer-literate. Numbers and examples, not
  superlatives. "Accept payments" before "unlock growth".

## Do / Don't
- Do: put proof where the doubt is, use diagrams to explain.
- Don't: copy the gradient header, the diagrams, or the copy.
- Take for your work: proof before pitch, colour with a meaning.

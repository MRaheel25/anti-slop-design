# Type Guide — Logo

## Craft rules

- A logo must work at 16px and on a sign. Test both before anything
  else. If it fails at 16px, simplify — do not add detail.
- One idea. A mark that tries to say three things says nothing.
  The idea should come from what the company does, its name, or
  its world — not from a generic symbol library.
- Draw in one colour first. Colour is a variant, not the idea. The
  mark must work in black, in white reversed, and in a single
  brand colour.
- Wordmark, monogram, icon, combination, or emblem — pick the type
  from the intake, and say why. A wordmark is right when the name
  itself is distinctive; an icon earns its place when it must be
  recognised without words.
- Letterforms: check spacing (kerning) by eye at large and small
  sizes. Check the exact spelling and case against the intake —
  a wrong letter is the one mistake that cannot be fixed later.
- Never redraw or edit a supplied logo. Never use a competitor's
  mark, a stock logo, or a trademarked symbol. Never invent a
  trademark check.
- Deliver variants, not one file: colour, mono, reversed, favicon /
  app icon, and a clear-space and minimum-size note.

## Anti-slop for logos

- No generic swoosh, globe, leaf, lightbulb, or handshake mark
  unless the brief's subject genuinely is that thing — and even
  then, find the specific version of it.
- No gradient fill as the idea. No stock icon with the company name
  typed underneath in a default font and called a logo.
- No thin, low-contrast mark that disappears at small sizes.

## Research notes for logos

Research brand identity case studies (Behance), award winners, and
the competitors' marks named in the intake. Extract: how the mark
reduces to one idea, how it scales, how it behaves in one colour.
Record what was not copied — especially the competitors' distinctive
shapes.

## Deliverable

Mark in SVG plus PNG exports, colour / mono / reversed variants,
clear-space and minimum-size guidance, and the one-sentence idea
behind the mark.
